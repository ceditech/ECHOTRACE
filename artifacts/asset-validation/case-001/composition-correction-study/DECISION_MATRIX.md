# Decision matrix and uncertainty

**Mandatory gate:** preserve exactly three canonical mystery events/IDs, closed shared wallet source, no new/removed story props, stable character treatment and stable environment at both endpoints. A1, A2 and B **PASS this structural/endpoint gate**. Passing does not certify clue recognizability; A2 is still unacceptable for presentation. A candidate failing the mandatory gate receives no weighted score. Baseline is a defect reference, not a candidate recommended for adoption.

## Proposed weights and scoring rubric

Use owner's requested weights: canonical/application regression20%, physical support/composition25%, mobile readability25%, touch correspondence/separation20%, complexity/maintainability10%. Total100%. Score each criterion on a coarse1–5 ordinal scale, with larger values better:1 severe observed defect;2 material unresolved deficit;3 mixed/conditional;4 well-supported offline result with remaining validation;5 very strong within the specific criterion and available evidence. A5 for unchanged canonical data does not imply a5 for human recognition.

Bands reflect uncertainty, not confidence intervals or measured probabilities. Weighted total is `sum(weight × score / 5)`, yielding broad review ranges out of100. It is an owner-priority aid, not manufactured scientific precision. Visual judgments and feature-size risks are explicitly different from objective byte/geometry tests.

## Acceptance independent of ranking

A1 is rejected for actual support. A2 is rejected for visibly tiny clues, partial support failure and larger effective table/occlusion changes. A low regression-risk score cannot compensate for those defects. B is the only tested candidate satisfying both the support screening goals and full320/390 target comfort checks while retaining useful clue area and original character treatment. It therefore earns **APPROACH B RECOMMENDED for the next owner design gate**.

This recommendation is conditional on owner acceptance of proposed geometry/art composition and character/table association. It does not convert static scores into physical plausibility proof, recognition success, accessibility compliance or production permission. No candidate is ready for automatic adoption. If owner requires exact canonical metadata and forbids all effective internal shifts, A1/A2 remain rejected; the result would be no accepted candidate under that additional constraint, requiring a separately authorized art treatment rather than silent B adoption.

Under these weights/bands B ranks above A1/A2, but changing the priority to canonical immutability could change a preference ranking without making unsupported A art acceptable. Device/human evidence is absent; B's readability score cannot be fixed to a precise outcome. Hybrid is not separately recommended: adding A2 table shifts to B increases risk and loses its existing-table advantage. Minor future art polish would be a new scoped art decision, not a proven hybrid solution.
