## Original passport and mat

Static inspection can distinguish a closed booklet against the mat, with gold page/cover edges supporting recognition; navy-on-navy material, small title/emblem and the new envelope's aspect normalization remain risks. Canonical envelope is approximately square, while the B-oriented candidate source is wider; assigning exact canonical envelope changes its raster proportions. We do not retouch or conceal that mismatch. No passport fine-print reading should be required.

The original passport and mat extend at/over the table front rim in this candidate/canonical render. This is a material support/perspective issue and may change how participants interpret the object relationship. Larger CSS dimensions are not evidence of a better composition. The remembered passport rectangle encloses the rendered solid passport at the canonical location and persists when the visual is absent; it is intentionally not a target covering every edge of the much larger mat. Original inspection itself has no selectable target.

## Numerals 12 and 21

Gold numerals on navy remain distinguishable in informed static inspection, especially at 390, but the numeral union is only ~9.55px high at 320. No first-time recognition success rate is inferred. Both stand bodies are shared, upright and identical outside numeral treatment; exchange is one event.

Canonical target bounds include the full solid stand, including bases, unlike the previously measured B base mismatch. Example at source 960: stand solid base reaches approximately y177 while its canonical region spans y54–216. Canonical target spacing is not the observed issue here. **Visual support is:** canonical stand centers y=.25 place this candidate stand base above the candidate tabletops. Both stands appear to float. This blocks visual/adoption acceptance, not mathematical membership testing. The study does not move stands to B's y=.335 or adopt B's narrow targets.

## Wallet and strap

Movement remains left/window to right/plant, with the same closed silhouette, star and broad alternating navy/ivory strap. Canonical rendering yields a taller-looking wallet than B because envelope width/height differs. Star/strap remain small and material glare competes with boundaries. The source feature-box measurement is not a contrast or accessibility compliance test. Individual stripes are narrower than the full strap band; do not equate band visibility with confident stripe identification.

The candidate wallet crosses/rests visually at the front rim under canonical coordinates. A correct hit at its canonical region does not demonstrate plausible contact or a fair spatial association. No artwork correction, opening, new emblem or highlighting is introduced. Owner must reconcile assets/staging with canonical coordinates, or approve a separately documented geometry change, before adoption.

## Characters and accessibility limits

Current testing Mara/Owen share identical source layer bytes, transforms and effective pixels across snapshots. Canonical props do not introduce a character endpoint difference. Their existing scale/depth/table association concerns remain; camera zoom scales people/tables equally and cannot fix ratios. Bright cream clothing and busy marble may increase attention demand. No name/face recognition is presumed at mobile scale.

External controls have keyboard focus and no motion transitions; a neutral keyboard cursor selects through the same regions. Diagnostics are research overlays, not participant cues. Screen-reader behavior, 200% text scaling, physical touch/tremor, pinch/native page zoom, contrast conformance and nonvisual equivalence were not tested. This is not an accessible completed game.

Conclusion: the prototype supports **actual-device technical diagnostic QA** and honest size/target inspection. It is **not** ready for production visual acceptance or a new controlled human recognition study. Floating/rim-crossing support and candidate envelope normalization need an owner decision, not a silent coordinate adjustment.
