# Design approach comparison

| Approach | Expected benefit, not proven | Memory/fairness costs | Geometry/authorization boundary | Recommendation |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| A. Readability polish in existing composition | Moderately reduce competing marble glare, preserve passport edge, simplify distracting texture around existing star/stripes; maintain stand shared master | Overemphasis can telegraph clues; contrast helps boundaries but does not enlarge 10px details. Character/table ratios remain | Asset derivatives need owner approval; no case coordinate change if dimensions, alpha silhouette and origins remain fixed. No retouch was executed here | Targeted second step after layout/selection diagnosis, not broad regeneration |
| B1. Responsive full-scene fit and wider landscape | Maximize available width; avoid decorative horizontal padding; controls outside scene; full panorama retained | Portrait still limited to 320/390 width; landscape is optional and must not become mandatory; orientation change mid-observation can disrupt timing | Presentation-only if uniform transform and coordinate mapping preserve all source geometry. B itself still needs independent adoption approval | Preferred baseline |
| B2. Taller camera / center crop | 533×300 scene gives 1.67× gain at 320 and 1.37× at 390 | Loses Mara/landmarks/backpack and clips left clue context. Pan/search consumes observation time; biased framing can hint or omit | Source geometry unchanged but default crop conflicts with full-scene observation rule; separate spec/product approval required | Reject as observation default; diagnostic only |
| C. Optional player zoom/pan, or user-centered magnifier | 2× enlarges passport, numerals and star; overview remains available. Useful for low-vision inspection and precise selection | Context lost locally; navigation costs attention; lens can occlude image; pinch-only controls exclude users. Timed observation use alters information access | Camera-only transforms don't require canonical coordinates, but phase availability and retention/reset policy require owner approval. No game/runtime system implemented | Prefer simple bounded zoom/pan in investigation over a floating lens; decide observation use separately |
| Combined B1 + limited A + optional C | Width first, restrained art readability, then user-initiated inspection | Must avoid changed-only enhancements, timer pauses, clue auto-centering, comparing original, or orientation/zoom disparity | Presentation/art decisions distinct from canonical geometry/hit-region changes | Smallest plausible direction; benefits remain unvalidated |

Artwork comparison is assessed from unchanged art and source measurements, not a rendered retouched candidate. Camera/zoom examples are actual uniformly transformed offline images. This deliberately avoids treating an invented polish result as evidence. No hypothesis has been human/device-validated here.

If zoom is allowed during observation, both snapshots need identical available controls and neutral limits. Original must still disappear at the normal deadline; zoom cannot pause/extend exposure, restore prior frames or provide saved original thumbnails. Start observation and investigation in the same full overview; transition clears original buffers visible to users and may reset the camera while blank. Preserve zoom/pan mappings within each endpoint. A persistent camera across endpoints is an alternative requiring explicit review because a participant may enter recall without seeing the destination table. Never auto-pan to a changed item.

A magnifier can preserve surrounding overview but occupies scarce mobile space and creates two representations at differing scales. A user-positioned lens must have identical behavior across endpoints, no clue anchoring/highlight, accessible single-pointer/button alternatives, and no persisted original lens content. It is more complex than ordinary zoom and is deferred.

Zoom/polish can combine with the memory mechanic if these rules hold; a default cropped camera cannot be silently combined under the current full-scene requirement. Improving table/person perspective cannot be achieved by a uniform camera transform. Reworking object size/position, or canonical target geometry, needs separately approved content changes. Changing only off-canonical character transforms needs a creative approval and clue/endpoint recheck; it is not an automatic case.json update.
